# Game concept landscape

This is a design-space record, not a promise that every idea should be built. Concepts are judged by the interaction they add, not by how easily they can be reskinned.

## Selection criteria

A strong SuperSimpleGames experiment should:

- give immediate, local feedback to ordinary and imprecise input;
- remain satisfying without reading, sound, speed, precision, or a correct answer;
- add a meaningfully different form of agency from existing games;
- keep motion, audio, state, and generated objects bounded;
- invite optional caregiver language or imitation without requiring adult instruction;
- expose a concept without claiming to teach or assess it;
- work offline with no permissions, accounts, remote assets, or personal data.

## Explored interaction models

| Model | New affordance | Promise | Principal risk | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stable pockets and doors | Open, close, revisit, remember | Containment and object permanence without a wrong choice | Can become merely three buttons if reveals lack character | **Build next** |
| Broad veil reveal | Gradually uncover one stable scene | Curiosity and persistence; every touch advances | Mechanically close to clearing another grid | Preserve |
| Stack and settle | Directly manipulate large pieces | Construction, size, position, simple planning | Collapse or rejected placement can feel like failure | Preserve |
| Free-drawn route | Create a path another object follows | Gesture, direction, trajectory, anticipation | Sustained dragging may exclude younger children; overlaps Bloom's drag surface | Preserve |
| Soft shape homes | Relate a piece to a matching place | Shape, size, containment, direct manipulation | A non-match easily becomes a visible wrong answer | Revisit after observation |
| Path-tile builder | Rotate pieces into a connected route | Strong spatial problem solving | Abstract, reversible progress and small cells skew older | Defer |
| Child-led rhythm | Create and replay a short sequence | Turn-taking and temporal order | Sound dependence, stimulation, and imitation pressure | Defer |
| Counting or tracing task | Reproduce a quantity or line | Familiar educational framing | Precision, language, correctness, and assessment conflict with the philosophy | Reject for this age slice |

## Decision: Peekaboo Pockets

The third experimental slice will show three oversized, visually distinct pockets. Each contains one familiar, stable object. Tapping a pocket opens or closes it; objects do not move between pockets or disappear. Every pocket responds, and there is no target to find. Discovering all three creates one brief acknowledgement but does not end play or replace the scene.

This adds a different state model from Bloom and Color Splash:

- Bloom: an action creates another visible object.
- Color Splash: an action transforms a connected region toward completion.
- Peekaboo Pockets: an action changes visibility while the hidden object's identity and location remain stable.

The smallest slice deliberately excludes shuffling, “wrong cup” play, collections, timers, levels, narrated questions, drag precision, and automatic new rounds. Caregivers may naturally name the objects or talk about open/closed and in/out; the app does not require or score that conversation.

## Evidence boundary

The [CDC's current two-year guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/toddlers-2-3-years.html) describes simple object manipulation, shape/color sorting, pretend play, and exploration as familiar activities. [NAEYC's technology guidance](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/topics/technology-and-media/infants-and-toddlers) emphasizes active, child-controlled exploration with an adult mediator. Current [American Academy of Pediatrics guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/helping-kids-thrive-in-a-digital-world-AAP-policy-explained.aspx) emphasizes quality, context, conversation, co-play, and protecting time for real-world interaction.

Those sources inform constraints; they do not establish that this game teaches object permanence or improves development. The only initial claims should concern observable interaction: whether children discover it, receive understandable feedback, revisit pockets, remain comfortable, disengage naturally, and invite shared attention with a caregiver.
