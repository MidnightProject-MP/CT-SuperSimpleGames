# Story Scenes formative playtest

## Purpose

Test whether young children can move from immediate default flower placement to deliberately choosing, placing, revisiting, and combining a tiny cast. This is an observation of interaction clarity and child-authored play, not an assessment of imagination, language, categorization, or narrative ability.

Use the consent, privacy, device-locking, assent, and immediate-stop rules in `PLAYTEST.md`. Story Scenes is available from the launcher, but treat four caregiver–child sessions as directional evidence only.

## Session

Allow up to five minutes and stop immediately when the child disengages or shows discomfort.

1. Open Story Scenes directly without explaining the palette. Flowers are already selected.
2. Wait roughly 15 seconds for a self-directed action.
3. If needed, offer once: “You can touch the garden.” Do not name a desired object or demonstrate a combination.
4. Observe whether the child continues placing flowers, selects another family, or touches an existing object.
5. If the child has not noticed the palette after several successful placements, the caregiver may say once: “There are more things here.” Do not point to a particular choice.
6. Do not demonstrate rainbows, watering, warming, greetings, or dragging. Record whether a relationship is encountered and then intentionally repeated.
7. The caregiver may narrate or take one natural turn only if the child invites shared play.

## Decision thresholds

Keep its product evidence at prototype status and revise one interaction variable unless:

- at least three of four children produce an immediate scene response without instruction or after one neutral invitation;
- ordinary scene taps and palette choices always have legible local feedback;
- at least some children select a second family and place it without a demonstrated two-step instruction;
- touching existing objects does not look like accidental deletion or failure;
- relationships remain calm and understandable when encountered, even if they are not deliberately sought;
- no child shows repeated frustration, sensory distress, unsafe navigation, or resisted disengagement.

Launcher promotion needs stronger evidence than first-use success: at least some children should voluntarily revisit the scene on another occasion or use its objects in clearly self-directed variation or shared storytelling. If selection repeatedly causes confusion, test a direct object-spawning palette before adding backgrounds or more content.
