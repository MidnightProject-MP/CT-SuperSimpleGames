# Together Tones formative playtest

## Purpose

Test whether four stable visual voices invite comfortable repetition, intentional variation, and informal turn-taking without sound dependence, imitation demands, or engagement pressure. This is not an assessment of rhythm, memory, music, attention, or cooperation.

Use the consent, privacy, device-locking, assent, and immediate-stop rules in `PLAYTEST.md`. Together Tones is available from the launcher, but treat four caregiver–child sessions as directional evidence only.

## Session

Allow up to four minutes and stop immediately when the child disengages or shows discomfort.

1. Open Together Tones without demonstrating a sequence.
2. Wait roughly 15 seconds for a self-directed action.
3. If needed, offer once: “You can touch one.” Do not name a preferred voice.
4. Observe whether the child repeats one voice, varies voices, or notices the changing link and bead trail.
5. After ordinary exploration, repeat a short portion with sound muted. Do not ask whether the child noticed the difference; observe whether the visual response remains sufficient.
6. The caregiver may take one natural turn if invited. Do not establish a required alternating rule or ask the child to copy a pattern.
7. End at the first clear stopping signal. Do not restart sound, introduce tempo, or demonstrate more combinations to regain attention.

## Decision thresholds

Keep its product evidence at prototype status and revise one interaction variable unless:

- at least three of four children activate a voice without instruction or after one neutral invitation;
- every ordinary, repeated, and rapid touch produces a legible bounded response;
- sound-off play remains understandable and satisfying;
- at least some children intentionally repeat or vary voices without being asked to copy;
- caregiver participation can occur naturally without turning the experience into instruction;
- no child shows sensory distress, apparent correctness pressure, unsafe navigation, or resisted disengagement.

Do not interpret long sessions, many taps, or copied sequences as stronger evidence. Launcher promotion requires comfortable agency, comprehensible visual feedback, and at least some voluntary later return—not performance.
